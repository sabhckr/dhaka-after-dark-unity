# Farmgate – Karwan Bazar uprising graffiti: evidence register

Research date: 3 Nov 2026. Read-only web research (WebSearch + WebFetch page text). No images or files were downloaded. Image URLs are links only.

## 0. Headline finding (read this first)

1. **Almost all the documented paintings on the MRT Line 6 viaduct pillars between Karwan Bazar and Farmgate are from July 2025, not July–August 2024.** They are a state-commissioned series titled "Revisiting the Fascist Regime" ("ফিরে দেখা ফ্যাসিস্ট রেজিম"). Adviser Asif Mahmud initiated it, Dhaka North City Corporation (DNCC) carried it out, and a team of about 50 painted it, including Dhaka University and Jagannath University fine-arts students. Painting started at Karwan Bazar around late June / early July 2025. Ittefaq reported the Karwan Bazar–Farmgate phase complete by 12 Jul 2025. The full Agargaon–Karwan Bazar series was inaugurated on 1 Aug 2025 as "July Art Work".
2. A bdnews24 photo (5 Jul 2025) shows **pillars being painted white as a base** before this series. bdnews24 (20 Jul 2025) says the citizens' artworks painted on metro pillars after 5 Aug 2024 were removed about a year later and replaced by the government series. That statement covers metro pillars in general and does not name any Farmgate or Karwan Bazar pillar.
3. **The only 2024 evidence for this area that I could find is not on the pillars.** NTV Online (9 Aug 2024) photographed student graffiti "on a road-divider wall" in the Farmgate area. DBC News (11 Aug 2024) says its reporters saw students painting graffiti at Farmgate, among several other areas. Neither source transcribes any artwork text, and neither gives a precise position.
4. Pillar numbers stated by sources: **464, 466 and 467 (Karwan Bazar area)**, plus **432/433 (near Farmgate station)**. Pillars 432/433 come from a bearing-pad accident report and have no graffiti connection.

If the game is set in July–August 2024, the 2025 pillar series is anachronistic. Treat it as "post-uprising commemoration" (from July 2025 onward), not as uprising-era graffiti.

## 3. What "confirmed" rests on

- **Cat 1 (Farmgate):** Only two Aug 2024 news items, NTV Online (S16) and DBC (S17), name Farmgate as a place where students painted. S16 gives a surface type, a road-divider wall; S17 gives nothing more. There is no pillar, no text and no precise position. Rows: **2** (one artwork group plus one activity record).
- **Cat 2 (Karwan Bazar):** Dhaka Tribune (S1) names pillars 464 (north side), 466 and 467 in the Karwan Bazar area. The Daily Star caption (S2) names "P-464 at Karwan Bazar", and S3/S4 repeat 464. The remaining rows rest on S1's "nearby metro pillars" in the Karwan Bazar area or S5's "Karwan Bazar metro pillars". Rows: **10** (GF-003 to GF-012). Three of these are successive states of one pillar face (464).
- **Cat 3 (nearby, uncertain):** The sources say "Karwan Bazar to Farmgate", "Farmgate through Manipuri Para to Agargaon" or "Agargaon to Karwan Bazar", with no pillar. Rows: **12** (GF-013 to GF-024).
- **Cat 4 (no location):** Rows: **3** (GF-025 to GF-027).

**Pillar-number reference points (stated by sources):** pillars **432/433** are near Farmgate Metro Station (S22, an accident report). Pillars **464, 466 and 467** are in the Karwan Bazar area (S1). *Inference only, not stated by any source:* the Farmgate→Karwan Bazar run therefore spans roughly the 430s to the 460s, with numbers increasing southward. The year sequence also appears to run backwards in pillar number: 467 = 2012 (Sagar-Runi), 466 = Shapla Chattar (2013), 464 = blogger killings and "Terrorism". This is consistent with a series that S3 says began at Kawran Bazar, but it is unverified. Do not place any other year on a pillar from this pattern without on-site confirmation.

## 4. Rights summary

**Openly licensed (photographs):**
- Wikimedia Commons, Hasnat Abdullah, "Student Movement Graffiti Dhaka 01–34": **CC BY-SA 4.0**. Commercial use is allowed with attribution, but derivatives must be shared under the same licence. A game texture derived from it would arguably need to be released CC BY-SA, which is usually unworkable for a commercial game. No location in the study area.
- Wikimedia Commons, DeloarAkram series: **CC BY 4.0** (attribution only). These are Mirpur and DU locations, so they are not in the study area.
- WallVoice24BD dataset (Mendeley): **CC BY 4.0** at dataset level. Per-image locations within the "Mirpur-Uttara-Tejgaon" folder are unverified.
- Commons station photos that might show pillars post-uprising: "ফার্মগেট মেট্রো স্টেশন.jpg" (S.M.M.Musabbir Uddin, CC BY-SA 4.0, 30 Sep 2024) and "কারওয়ান বাজার মেট্রোরেল স্টেশন ২০২৪.jpg" (Ronju65, CC BY 4.0, 21 Sep 2024). Their content was not inspected.

**Press photos, no licence stated (all rights reserved by default):** Daily Star (Star; Prabir Das), bdnews24 (Abdullah al Momin; others uncredited), UNB (Tahiyat Nazifa Noor), NTV Online, Dhaka Tribune ("Collected"), Ekushey TV, Voice7 ("Collected"), Ittefaq ("Collected"), DBC. Two images are credited "PID" (Press Information Department): the Financial Express and New Age inauguration photos. The licence status of PID images was not verified here. Several images are "Collected" or come from the adviser's Facebook, so the true copyright owner is unknown.

**The artworks themselves:**
- The 2025 pillar series is a DNCC/LGRD-commissioned work by a named-only-collectively team (Md Shahjahan oversaw it, per S1). Who holds copyright (the individual artists, DNCC or the ministry) is not stated anywhere I found.
- The 2024 student graffiti is anonymous.
- A photograph's CC licence covers only the photo, not the underlying mural. I did not verify whether Bangladeshi copyright law has a freedom-of-panorama exception for 2D works in public places.
- Several pieces depict real, identifiable people: murdered bloggers, Khaleda Zia, Nahid Islam, Sarjis Alam, and victims Abrar, Felani and Tonu. They carry politically contested claims, and one piece has already been defaced.

**For commercial reproduction in a game, you would need:**
1. Written permission from the photographer or publisher for any press photo used as reference or texture source.
2. For close reproduction of a specific mural, permission from its artist(s) or DNCC, or a legal opinion on public-art exceptions in Bangladesh.
3. Review of likeness and defamation exposure for portraits of real people.

The safest route is original artwork that follows the documented *format* (year face plus three event faces on a white base, or the freehand 2024 student style) without copying a specific photographed panel. This is a research note, not legal advice.

## 5. What is missing

- **2024 pillar evidence for this stretch: none found.** No source shows or describes uprising-era (Jul–Aug 2024) graffiti on any pillar between Farmgate and Karwan Bazar. bdnews24's general statement that post-5-Aug-2024 citizen pillar art was removed after a year, plus the 5 Jul 2025 white base-coat photo (S5, photo 10), suggest any such work was overpainted in July 2025. Neither source names a Farmgate or Karwan Bazar pillar. The blocked bd-pratidin piece (Nov 2024, "graffiti overlaid by posters") and Prothom Alo's 7 Aug 2024 photo story are the next places to check. The Feb 2025 poster survey (S18) lists only posters at Farmgate pillars, which suggests there was no prominent graffiti there then.
- **To place each artwork on a pillar face, you would need:**
  1. On-site or dated street-level photos showing the stencilled pier number together with the panel. Mapillary or Google Street View captures after Jul 2025 for the Karwan Bazar–Farmgate stretch, and pre-Jul-2025 captures for the 2024 state.
  2. A DNCC or project list mapping year to pillar number (the 66-pillar plan in S1).
  3. Compass orientation of each face. S1 gives "northern side" only for 464.
  4. For GF-001, the exact divider segment: NTV's 10 photos would need landmarks identified (Ananda Cinema, Khamarbari, the Farmgate foot-over-bridge, Tejgaon College).
  5. Size: no source gives any dimensions.
