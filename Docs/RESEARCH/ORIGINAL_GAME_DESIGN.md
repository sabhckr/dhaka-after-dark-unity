# Original game design

First draft, 2026-10-30. The protagonist's name is **Isaac**, the owner's decision of 2026-11-10 (the draft called him Rafiq "Rafi"); the family surname Mondol is kept from the draft until the owner says otherwise. Every character, organisation, station and mission here is invented for this game. No real living person, political party or company is depicted. The research files ([VICE_CITY_DESIGN_RESEARCH.md](VICE_CITY_DESIGN_RESEARCH.md), [FEATURE_TRANSLATION_MATRIX.md](FEATURE_TRANSLATION_MATRIX.md)) inform the structure; nothing is taken from Vice City's plot, characters, missions, dialogue, map or music.

Claims about Dhaka life are kept general. Anything that could be wrong or sensitive is marked **(check)**, to be confirmed with Dhaka residents before content is written.

## Premise

Dhaka never sleeps, and the night belongs to whoever controls the roads.

**Isaac Mondol**, 27, comes home from three years of construction work abroad with savings and a plan. His father ran a small transport business: two CNG auto-rickshaws and a microbus route licence. Isaac finds it gone. His father signed it over to clear a debt to the **Talukder Line**, a syndicate that collects "line money" from drivers at terminals and junctions across the city, and the old man now drives one of those CNGs as a hired night driver.

Isaac's first goal is small and concrete: **get the family's vehicles back.** The game is about what that goal turns into.

## Tone

- An urban crime drama with warmth and humour. Family, friendship and the city's resilience matter as much as the crime.
- The night is the game's identity: the hours when the markets wholesale, the traffic thins into speed, the power cuts out and the city shows its other face.
- Violence has consequences. The protagonist is capable but not a superhero.

| **Sumon "Lal" Kabir** | The Line's enforcer at Kawran Bazar | First antagonist; brutal, funny, insecure |
| **Jahangir Talukder** | Head of the Talukder Line; public face of a "transport welfare" association in Gulshan | Main antagonist; patient, charming, untouchable until he isn't |

## Structure: three acts, soft gating

The whole map is drivable from the start. **Content is gated, not roads:** each act opens new mission givers, businesses and police responses in its districts. Gulshan and Banani respond faster and harder to crime from the start.

| Act | Districts | Spine | Ends with |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. *Night Shift* | Farmgate, Kawran Bazar, Tejgaon and its rail corridor | Drive fares, learn the city; meet Bablu; collide with Lal over line money; win back the first CNG | Lal humiliated in front of the market; the Line notices Isaac |
| 2. *Routes* | Motijheel, Old Dhaka, Mirpur, Dhanmondi, Shahbagh | Build a rival night cooperative: drivers who pay into a fund instead of to the Line. Businesses open (garage, tea stalls, the microbus route, Nasrin's workshop). Moushumi digs into the Line's finances | Choice 1: build the case with SI Kamrul, or take Faria Talukder's offer of her uncle's ledgers in exchange for a share of the terminals |
| 3. *High Water* | Gulshan, Banani, Uttara, airport road, the river edges | Monsoon. Isaac learns the Line's terminal money funds a land-filling scheme on the wetlands at the city's edge (fictional company **Meghna Rivers Holdings**) **(check: keep the depiction general)** | Choice 2 decides the ending |

## Endings (from the two choices and the cooperative's reputation)

- **On air.** Isaac gives Moushumi the evidence; the story breaks live on Raat FM during the flood night; Talukder falls through the courts. The cooperative survives, smaller and clean.
- **The new Line.** Isaac takes Talukder's terminals for himself, with Faria's deal. Rich and alone; Nasrin stops speaking to him.
- **The last fare.** Isaac gets the family out of the fight: the vehicles are home, Abba retires, and Isaac drives the airport road one last time before dawn.

The reputation value is the cooperative's standing: fair fares, protected drivers, no extortion. It is built through side jobs, not dialogue choices.

## The city as the game

| System | Dhaka identity (original design) |
|---|---|
| **Vehicles** | CNG auto-rickshaw (light, nimble, fragile), cycle rickshaw (slow, squeezes through lanes, pedalled), private cars, microbus, city bus (heavy, wide turns), intercity coach, motorcycle, pickup, ambulance, police pickup. Each has its own spec asset |
| **Traffic** | Dense and mixed, with rule-bending as normal behaviour: lane straddling, honking as communication, buses stopping anywhere near a stop. Traffic police direct some junctions by hand alongside signals **(check which junctions)** |
| **Heat (wanted system)** | Five levels: witnesses → local police on foot or motorbike → patrol pickups → checkpoints on arterials → armed response units. Every exit is a place on the map: blend into a crowded market lane on foot, re-plate and repaint at Bablu's garage, or change clothes at Nasrin's workshop or a tailor stall. Paying a fine clears level 1. No bribe pickups: the research flags them as a stereotype about local police. Gulshan raises response by one level |
| **Respect** | Mosques, temples, churches and other religious sites are no-combat zones: weapons holster and heat cannot rise inside them. No killing-spree challenges; timed challenges are non-lethal or against the Line's crews only |
| **Night events** | Load-shedding: a district's power cuts out, streetlights go dark and generators hum; some missions use it. Pre-dawn wholesale at Kawran Bazar. Monsoon nights: waterlogged roads slow vehicles and stall small engines |
| **Side jobs** | CNG fares; Kawran Bazar pre-dawn deliveries; ambulance runs through jams; waterlogging rescues; rickshaw races through Old Dhaka lanes; microbus route shifts; collectible rickshaw-art panels hidden around the city |
| **Businesses** | Garage (vehicle storage, repaint), tea stall chain (small steady income, gossip that reveals side jobs), microbus route (income that depends on cooperative reputation), Nasrin's workshop (story-locked, clean money), a rooftop flat in Dhanmondi (safehouse) |
| **Radio** | Raat FM (talk, news and the story's call-in show), Dhaka Beat (Bangla hip-hop), Shohor Rock, Nodi (folk-inspired), Tin Shed (electronic), Purono Diner Gaan (classic style). All music must be commissioned originals or licensed tracks. Talk segments are written for this game |
| **Time and weather** | Full 24-hour cycle, but the story and the best content are at night. Seasons are compressed: dry winter haze in Act 1, pre-monsoon storms in Act 2, monsoon in Act 3 |

## Mission design rules

1. **Every mission teaches or tests one system** and uses a real place in the data (a place node or landmark id from `places.json` / `landmarks.json`).
2. **Checkpoints at every major beat.** A retry restarts at the last checkpoint with the state from that point.
3. **Failure is a story beat where possible.** A failed delivery costs money and reputation instead of a blank restart, if the mission allows it.
4. **No mission longer than about 12 minutes** without a checkpoint **(target, to be tested in playtests)**.
5. **Side content feeds the spine:** cooperative reputation, money for businesses, and contacts who appear in main missions.
